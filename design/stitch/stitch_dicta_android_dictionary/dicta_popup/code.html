TO THE LIGHTHOUSE CHAPTER III • 42

The sky was still of a pale, luminous violet, trembling on the verge of twilight. She sat quiet on the terrace, watching the tide slip between the black ribs of the pier with a gentle, monotonous hiss. Nothing remained fixed in this soft dusk; memory itself seemed to dissolve into vapor, leaving only the shadow of emotions once felt with startling clarity.

What was love then? It was not a marble monument nor a promise cast in bronze, but something vastly more ephemeral — a phosphorescent gleam on the crested wave, sparkling for an unrepeatable second before breaking against the dark shingle. Even so, the sheer fragility of the moment rendered it sacred, elevated far above the heavy permanence of stone and iron.

She watched the lighthouse beam revolve slowly across the bay, sweeping its golden fan across the waves, bathing the solitary rocks in silent reassurance before dipping again into the deep nocturnal abyss.
